# Fraser Price's BRRR bench on vLLM-Moet's served DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash — 2026-11-03 (`-r23`, the prefill front)

The same unmodified `src/brrr/bench.py` of [`fraserprice/DeepSeek-4.1-Flash-1017-BRRR`](https://huggingface.co/fraserprice/DeepSeek-4.1-Flash-1017-BRRR)
(sha256 `f0248bdd842b…`, invoked through the two-line runner of [the 2026-10-28 record](brrr-dsv41-2026-10-28.md)), run against
production ten minutes after the switch to image `-r23` — the week's prefill front: step 40 (the sparse-MLA prefill on FlashInfer's
full-BF16 kernel), step 41 (sequence-parallel mHC boundaries) and step 42 (the prefill MoE on our bit-identical FC1 / FC2 kernels),
plus the deterministic indexer top-k (step 26) served on since 2026-11-02. Defaults as before: calibrated code prompt of
2048 / 16384 / 65528 tokens, five rounds per cell, temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0, thinking off, generation to natural EOS capped at
2,048 tokens, the hottest GPU cooled to 70 °C before every round; PP from a `max_tokens=1` pass, TG from a prefix-cached second
pass timed while every stream decodes.

A week ago the prefill was the one column where the recipe led (2–6 % at 16K–65K, 27 % on the launch-bound 2K prompts). Steps
40–42 moved it: single-stream prefill is now 11–22 % faster than the recipe's and the aggregate prefill at 4–8 streams 19–28 %
faster; against our own `-r21-mxfp8` the gain is 18–51 % (the 2K prompts gained most: the sequence-parallel boundaries and the
MoE kernels both shorten the per-chunk fixed cost). The fresh 163K prefill measured by `prefill_time.py` tells the same story:
9.0 s (`-r21-mxfp8`, 2026-10-29) → 7.66 s (`-r22`) → **7.17–7.21 s (`-r23`, 22.6–22.8K tok/s)**.

Decode (TG) is untouched by construction and the bench agrees within its spread: single stream 776 / 758 / 756 tok/s at
2K / 16K / 65K (766 / 750 / 744 on 2026-10-28), eight streams 1,875 / 1,857 / 1,794 aggregate (1,960 / 1,937 / 1,866 — 3–4 % lower;
the deterministic indexer top-k now served costs the decode step more at long contexts — 44 vs 15 µs per call at 100K
columns in the 2026-10-21 microbench, eight calls per step — which is the one thing to re-measure; the Fraser bench on
short prompts was 129.3 steps/s the same night, equal to `-r22`'s 129.9). The acceptance (5.96–6.01 tokens per step at
temperature 1.0) is unchanged.
