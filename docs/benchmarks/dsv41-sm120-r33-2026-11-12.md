# DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on sm_120: the served image from `-r32` to `-r33` — 2026-11-12

What was measured on the reference host when the served image changed from `-r32` to `-r33`: the same series on
the old server and on the new one within one hour, the bits, a kernel check on the four cards, what the new server
logged, four paired series of runtime keys inside the serving server — the second attention cubin against the
shipped one, the m-tile loops, an FC2 grid cap, the m-tile loops under that cap —, where the pipelined step waits,
the selection of the second attention cubin with the bits in that state, the host memory, and — forty minutes
after the new server became ready — a series on it alone, which gives its time against `-r32`. What the image adds
is in the [changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md); the switches and their served state are in
[`docs/dsv41-sm120.md`](../dsv41-sm120.md#switches).

- **The limit of the change** is on the new server's 163K median: at most 1.02 × the old image's median with no
  other request in flight, 5 442 ms
  ([the 2026-11-11 record](dsv41-sm120-r32-2026-11-11.md#the-new-server-alone)). The series of this table does not
  decide it. It reads 5 555 ms, in the environment state, with up to three requests in flight at once while it was
  measured, and a series beside other requests is not comparable with one that ran alone: the old server itself,
  which read those 5 442 ms alone and minutes old, reads 5 721.5 ms in this hour, a day old, on warmer cards and
  beside other requests. The series that decides is [the new server alone](#the-new-server-alone): 5 287.5 ms.
- **Decode**: the new server's Fraser series ran minutes after it became ready, behind its prefill series; nine of
  its ten runs read 127.0 – 127.8 steps/s and one 121.0. A young server measured in this place has read below a
  long-running one at every change of image so far ([the 2026-11-11 record](dsv41-sm120-r32-2026-11-11.md)).
- **Under CPU load**: the three runs under 100 nice'd CPU hogs read 5 879 – 5 933 ms.

## The runtime keys in the serving server

Four series, one after the other in one lifetime of the new server, each a paired comparison of two states of
step 42b's runtime keys. A pair is two units, one per state; 8 valid pairs per series, four in the order AB and
four BA. A unit with another request in flight voids its pair, which is repeated (4, 2, 2 and 1 pairs voided);
the state of every unit is confirmed on the four ranks, and after each series every key is back in its
environment state. The effect is read by order: in every series the second unit of a pair is slower than the
first, by 0.40 – 0.55 % at 163K.

## Where the pipelined step waits

Before the four series the phase-wait instrument of step 47 was on for two units through its runtime key (level
3; 184 instrument lines, a full reading on 4 of 4 ranks). Per sequence-parallel layer, ms; a cell with two values
is the slot beside the second group's MoE and the slot beside the first group's:

| rank | steps | compute work | comm work | period | the comm phase beside a MoE | the MoE segment | the compute stream waits behind the phase | replay tail per step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 35 | 9.230 | 10.294 | 10.816 | 2.903 / 2.901 | 2.331 / 2.314 | 0.571 / 0.589 | 20.70 |
| 1 | 37 | 7.890 | 10.352 | 10.814 | 2.896 / 2.922 | 1.941 / 1.876 | 0.955 / 1.046 | 20.69 |
| 2 | 37 | 9.045 | 10.298 | 10.814 | 2.870 / 2.902 | 2.286 / 2.261 | 0.585 / 0.641 | 20.71 |
| 3 | 37 | 8.073 | 10.341 | 10.821 | 2.874 / 2.920 | 2.008 / 1.948 | 0.866 / 0.974 | 20.71 |

On every rank the comm work of a layer is longer than its compute work, and the compute stream waits behind the
comm phase after every MoE: the comm stream sets the pace of the MoE slots, also on the rank with the longest MoE
segment (rank 0: 2.323 ms, 21.7 % longer than rank 1's; its smallest wait 0.571 ms). For the grid caps of step 42
the instrument's reading is that a cap can shorten a MoE slot: the smallest wait behind a MoE, 0.571 ms, is larger
than what a cap adds to the MoE segment (0.05 ms at 320 CTAs, 0.11 at 256, 0.25 at 152), and the glue of a comm
phase is 0.622 ms longer beside a MoE than beside an attention — the most a cap can give back.

## The second cubin as served

After the four series the second cubin was selected for serving, on the condition that its series reads "faster"
by order at both prompt lengths: the key `SPARSE_MLA_PREFILL_SASS_CUBIN_SEL` was written as 1, the change confirmed
on the four ranks, long_agree and agree taken in that state ([Bits](#bits)), and the control file removed. A
removed file keeps the state it last set: the four ranks report that key at 1 and every other key at its
environment's value, and no control file exists. The state belongs to the running server: a restart comes up in
the environment state, on the shipped cubin with `v43c` loaded beside it.

## The new server alone

Two series on the new server as it serves — the second cubin selected — about 40 and 50 minutes after it became
ready: pairs of a 19K and a 163K fresh prefill back to back, a run counted only when no other request was in flight
during it (the count in flight sampled at 5 Hz). A third series between the two met other clients' requests in each
of its three 163K runs; those are left out.

| what | clean runs | median | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| fresh prefill, 163K tokens | 12 | 5 287.5 ms (30.8K tok/s) | 5 205 – 5 339 ms |
| fresh prefill, 19K tokens | 15 | 651 ms (29.8K tok/s) | 639 – 666 ms |
| the hottest card when a 163K run starts | 12 | 58 °C | 49 – 63 °C |

Against the old image, from its clean runs with the same tool and the same rule:

| the old server's runs | clean 163K runs | median | the new server against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| at the same age, half an hour after it became ready | 4 | 5 409 ms | −2.25 % |
| its first 4.5 hours | 25 | 5 369 ms | −1.52 % |
| its first 4.5 hours, at the same temperature of the hottest card | 25 | — | −144 ms ± 9 = −2.65 % (95 %: −3.00 … −2.30) |

A back-to-back series warms the cards, and each run is slower than the one before it. The new server's runs began
warmer (the hottest card at a median of 58 °C against 53 °C), so the medians understate the difference; the last
row is one least-squares line over both groups, 11.5 ms per °C of the hottest card at a run's start. It agrees with
the paired series above — the second cubin against the shipped one in one server, −2.569 % ± 0.323 % — and with
`-r33` running `-r32`'s kernels bit for bit while the shipped cubin serves. The limit of the change, 1.02 × the
5 442 ms above, is met.
