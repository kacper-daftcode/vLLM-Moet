# DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on 4× RTX 5090 + 512 GB RAM — the MoET hybrid, measured (2026-11-03/04)

The official `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` checkpoint (552B MoE, MXFP4 experts, 40 layers × 384 experts, a 189 GiB Engram
table) served at **native precision** on a workstation the model does not fit: four GeForce RTX 5090 (32 GB each, 128 GB in
all) hold the dense weights, the KV cache and **110 of the 384 experts of every MoE layer per TP rank**; every other routed
expert of a decode step is **computed on the CPU** (one CCD per TP rank, Threadripper PRO 9975WX) from the checkpoint's own bytes
pinned in host RAM, inside the captured CUDA graph, and its rows are injected into the GPU's own MoE kernel before the top‑k sum —
so the output is **bit‑identical to the all‑resident GPU path** (the design: [`docs/moet-hybrid.md`](../moet-hybrid.md)).

This page is the measurement record behind the README's section: our numbers, the same community benchmark script on the
native 4× RTX PRO 6000 configurations, and the other published ways to run this model below the datacenter.

|---|---|---|---|
| 2,050 | 0.87 s | 2,362 | 82.6 |
| 8,183 | 1.77 s | 4,621 | 83.7 |
| 32,767 | 10.04 s | 3,264 | 73.2 |
| 65,342 | 13.97 s | 4,677 | 69.7 |
| 129,571 | 28.00 s | 4,627 | 86.7 |
| 262,539 | 55.41 s | 4,738 | 84.7 |
| 511,998 | 112.8 s | 4,540 | 82.3 (256 tokens, streamed) |

Why the decode does not fall with the context: DeepSeek-V4.1's attention is sparse (an indexer picks the top‑512 tokens per
query; the KV of 18 layers is compressed 2×; part of the layers use a 128-token sliding window), so a decode step reads a
bounded slice of the KV regardless of its length. Why the prefill is flat at ~4.6K tok/s from 8K up: a 4096-token chunk routes
to ~84 % of all experts, so every chunk streams ~210 of the 384 experts per layer per rank (~160 GB over the four PCIe links)
through the GPU scratch — the prefill is the expert bytes, not attention. The 2K prompt is a single chunk whose streaming is
not amortised; the 32K sample is the slowest of the series (one boot, one slice of text — the band is ±10 %).

### The decode step (torch profiler, 40 steps of 'def fib(n):', rank 0)

7.5–7.6 ms per step: b12x MoE 3.1 ms, **waiting for the CPU tier 2.2 ms** (40 waits × 56 µs; the ~10 layers per step that
miss an expert pay that expert's full CPU latency, ~130 µs each — the GPU work between the router and the top‑k sum is only
31 µs), mHC / norms 1.25 ms, attention 1.1 ms, NCCL 0.75–0.96 ms (81 all-reduces), dense GEMVs 0.7 ms. Zero wait would be
~160 tok/s on this prompt; the CPU kernel is at 128–134 µs per 4.7 MB expert slice against a 69 µs compute floor and an 82 µs
per-CCD DRAM-bandwidth floor.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Every stream brings its own expert misses (concurrent sequences do not share them: 1.01 tokens per CPU-computed expert at 4
streams), so the hybrid serves one stream fast and several streams correctly but not much faster in aggregate — the CPU tier is
the bottleneck from two streams up. `--max-num-seqs 1` is the serving default for that reason.

The two PRO 6000 rows hold the whole model in 384 GB of VRAM and decode with a 7-token speculative draft (5.3–6 tokens per
step); the hybrid holds 29 % of the experts in 128 GB of VRAM, decodes one token per step and pays the CPU tier for the misses.
On this script's prompt the hybrid decodes at **93–94 tok/s on one stream regardless of the prompt length** (the sampled,
2,048-token code generation at temperature 1.0 routes to more distinct experts than the greedy short prompts of the headline
table — 94 vs 125 tok/s is the hit rate, not the context), **135–166 tok/s aggregate at 4–8 streams**, and prefills at
2.9–4.5K tok/s. Read the table as the price of 3× less GPU: ~1/8 of the native single-stream decode (which also has the
speculative draft's ~6 tokens per step) and ~1/4 of the prefill, at the same precision and context.

What the table says about the hybrid: among the systems that keep the model below a 384 GB-VRAM machine, it is the only one that
(a) keeps the experts at their released precision **and** (b) decodes above 100 tok/s — the CPU/GPU hybrids that compute all or
most experts on the CPU land at 20–50 tok/s on much weaker CPUs (Zen 2 / Zen 3 DDR4 hosts, AVX2) and the disk-streaming
single-GPU ports at 5–30; the native 4× PRO 6000 machines are 5× faster on one stream for 3× the GPUs. Its prefill (4.6K
tok/s) is 3× the strongest CPU-expert hybrid's and ~1/4 of native. Its weaknesses are also in the table: it needs 512 GB of host
RAM today (the Engram tables and the whole expert store pinned). The lower-RAM tiers exist on the branch `moet-hybrid-engram-v2`
(2026-11-04, measured on this box, not yet merged): the Engram tables on NVMe through io_uring with a DRAM row cache → **271 GB of
host RAM with decode unchanged** (144.5 / 124.9 tok/s, a mixed-prompt soak at 84.6 tok/s, TTFT +0.1–0.5 s); the cold experts on
NVMe behind a DRAM LRU → **194 GB at 130 / 108 tok/s** (TTFT 2.1–2.2× longer) and **106 GB at 111 / 82 tok/s** (TTFT 3.3–3.6×), the
within-boot identity check passing at every tier. It is CPU-bound from two concurrent streams, and it does not use
speculative decoding (a verify step routes 2–5× more distinct experts to the CPU, measured on the routing traces). On the BRRR
script's sampled code generation it decodes at 93–94 tok/s on one stream, i.e. ~2× the strongest published CPU-expert hybrid
and ~1/8 of the native machines.

## Reproduce

```bash
# the serving configuration (the launcher's defaults; the baked image hyb2)
MODEL_DIR=/mnt/nvme/models/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash docker/sm120/run-dsv41-5090.sh
# one measurement window under the box's GPU locks: identity prompts, decodes, the profile, TTFT / decode behind context
flock /tmp/exl3diff-gpu0.lock flock /tmp/exl3diff-gpu1.lock flock /tmp/exl3diff-gpu2.lock flock /tmp/exl3diff-gpu3.lock \
  bash tools/dsv41_5090/cpu_tier/tp4_window.sh k0-flag-ctx PREFIX_CACHE=0 DROP_CACHES=1 PROFILE_STEPS=40 \
  TTFT_LENGTHS=2048,8192,32768,65536,131072,262144 TTFT_REPEAT=1
# concurrency soaks with the thermal trace; the paired quality probes; the needles
... k0-flag-c2 MAX_NUM_SEQS=2 SOAK_MINUTES=10 SOAK_CONC=2     # and MAX_NUM_SEQS=4 SOAK_CONC=4
... k0-flag-quality HARD_PROBE=humaneval,math500
... k0-512k NEEDLE_LENGTHS=65536,262144                        # needle_probe.py --lengths 520000 for the ~512K set
# the BRRR bench: a serve with --max-num-seqs 8 and --enable-prompt-tokens-details, prefix caching on, then Fraser's bench.py
MAX_NUM_SEQS=8 EXTRA_ARGS="--enable-prompt-tokens-details" MODEL_DIR=/mnt/nvme/models/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash docker/sm120/run-dsv41-5090.sh
python -u brrr_run.py -p 8041 -s 2048 16384 -c 1 4 8 -r 3 && python -u brrr_run.py -p 8041 -s 65528 -c 1 4 -r 3
```

## The merged image

The numbers above were taken on the branch's own image (`…-hyb2`, built from `moet-hybrid` @ `b8ec181` before `main` was
merged in). The merged tree (`main` + the hybrid as Dockerfile step 43, image `…-r23-hyb`) reproduces them on the same box,
2026-11-04 02:11–03:03 UTC: the within-boot identity check **0 mismatches** (19,040 compact calls per rank, 95,314 GPU + 24,926
CPU rows), decode **146.3 / 125.0 tok/s**, the CPU-tier wait 2.19 ms per step, TTFT 0.87 / 3.30 / 6.80 s at 2K / 14K / 30K with the
image's `B12X_DYNAMIC_DETERMINISTIC_OUTPUT=1` (the PRO 6000 line's choice) and **0.87 / 3.29 / 6.51 s with it off** — the launcher's
`MOE_DETERMINISTIC` knob defaults to 0 for that 4 % at long prompts (decode 146.4 / 126.2, unchanged).

## Caveats

- One box, one day: the same-configuration boot-to-boot band on decode is ±3.5 % (eleven boots); single-sample prefill cells
  vary ±10 % with the text (the 32K sample above). The bands, not the last digit, are the claim.
- The published numbers of the other systems were measured by their authors with their own prompts and protocols (prose vs
  code, output lengths from 128 to 2,048 tokens, fresh vs resident content); they are quoted to place the hybrid, not to rank
  the others against each other.
- The 400 W power cap costs the hybrid ~2 % of TTFT at long prompts and nothing on decode; the native PRO 6000 rows were also
  measured at 400 W (325 W for the Max-Q recipe).
